# Communicators & AI Safety Kenya

An emerging community initiative helping Kenyan journalists, marketers and communications professionals understand catastrophic AI risks and explore pathways to contribute to AI safety.

## 1. What the initiative is

**Communicators & AI Safety Kenya** is a proposed community initiative exploring the role that communications professionals can play in understanding and responding to catastrophic risks from increasingly capable AI systems.

The initiative will begin with an online meetup bringing together journalists, marketing professionals, PR and corporate communications practitioners, researchers, digital media professionals and other communicators interested in AI safety.

The goal is not to turn participants into AI safety experts in a single event. Instead, it is to create an accessible entry point into the field, introduce participants to the core questions around catastrophic AI risk, and help them identify ways to continue learning or contribute using their existing skills.

The initiative is intended to be open and community-oriented rather than a commercial activity.

## 2. Why communications professionals?

AI safety is often discussed primarily as a technical or policy challenge. These areas are essential, but reducing catastrophic AI risk also requires people who can help different communities understand what increasingly capable AI systems could mean.

Journalists, science and technology communicators, marketers, PR professionals, researchers and digital media professionals already work at the intersection of information, organisations and audiences. They regularly translate complex issues, communicate uncertainty, scrutinise claims, shape organisational responses and navigate rapidly changing information environments.

These skills can complement technical research, governance and security work by helping connect AI safety knowledge with organisations, policymakers, media and the wider public.

The initiative therefore explores communications as a potential pathway into the broader AI safety ecosystem, without suggesting that communications can replace technical or policy approaches to AI safety.

## 3. Why catastrophic AI risk?

The initiative focuses specifically on **catastrophic AI risk** rather than general AI awareness or everyday AI harms.

As AI systems become increasingly capable, researchers and organisations are considering whether future systems could create risks on a much larger scale, including risks arising from loss of control, misuse of advanced capabilities and failures in how powerful AI systems are developed or deployed.

These questions are increasingly important, yet discussions about catastrophic AI risk remain concentrated within relatively small technical, research and policy communities.

The initiative aims to create another entry point into these discussions, particularly for professionals in Kenya who may already be engaging with AI but have had limited opportunities to encounter the catastrophic-risk perspective.

## 4. Who the event is for

The initial meetup will primarily target professionals in Kenya working in:

* Journalism and news media
* Science and technology communication
* Marketing
* Public relations and corporate communications
* Digital and social media
* Market and consumer research
* Public-interest and NGO communications
* Technology and innovation communications
* Communications strategy and community building

The event will be open to people with different levels of AI knowledge. Participants do not need previous experience in AI safety.

## 5. Proposed event format

The initial event will be a **60–90 minute online community meetup**.

A proposed structure:

1. **Welcome and context** — Why are we talking about catastrophic AI risk now?
2. **Introduction to AI safety** — Core concepts and the catastrophic-risk perspective.
3. **AI safety × communications** — Exploring how journalists, communicators and other professionals can contribute.
4. **Moderated discussion** — What does increasingly capable AI mean for our professions and wider society?
5. **Pathways for further engagement** — Ways participants can continue learning or contribute to AI safety.
6. **Closing and next steps** — Follow-up resources and opportunities for continued community engagement.

Where possible, the event will include a communications professional with experience in AI safety or someone working at the intersection of AI safety and public communication.

## 7. What participants will learn

By the end of the meetup, participants should have:

* A clearer understanding of what catastrophic AI risk means.
* An introduction to some of the central questions in AI safety.
* A better understanding of how catastrophic AI risk differs from more familiar discussions about current AI harms.
* An appreciation of the potential role of communications, journalism and media in the broader AI safety ecosystem.
* Practical examples of how communicators can engage with AI safety without overstating or sensationalising risks.
* Awareness of opportunities and resources for continuing to learn about AI safety.

## 8. Potential contribution pathways

The event will introduce participants to different ways of engaging with AI safety based on their existing skills and interests.

Potential pathways include:

* AI safety research
* AI evaluations and red-teaming
* AI security
* AI governance and policy
* AI safety communications
* Science and technology journalism
* Research communication
* Community building
* AI safety education and outreach
* Supporting organisations working on AI safety

The intention is to show that participants do not necessarily need to become machine learning researchers to begin contributing to the broader AI safety ecosystem.

## 9. How people can get involved

* Joining future discussions or reading groups.

The project is currently being developed as an open community initiative, and suggestions for improving the format and reaching relevant communities are welcome.
